## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Structured Data Extraction

**Description:**
The AI system reads a full Indonesian/English financial PDF (annual report, quarterly filing, or financial statement from an IDX-listed company) that has been pre-uploaded to the Gemini Files API, and generates a schema-validated JSON object containing: a 1–10 overall assessment score, four dimension sub-scores (Profitability, Balance Sheet, Growth Trend, Valuation Context), a one-sentence reasoning summary per dimension, and 2–3 verbatim quoted snippets per dimension with page citations. The output is inserted into the existing document analysis pipeline and displayed as a "verdict at a glance" score card in the document reader UI. The user population is urban Indonesian retail investors who cannot read financial statements; the AI score gives them a structured entry point before they read the plain-English explanation.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **Schema validation failure with no retry** — Gemini returns malformed JSON that fails Zod parse, causing an unhandled error that propagates as a hard fail and blocks document completion.
2. **Hallucinated citations** — AI cites page N but that page does not contain the quoted snippet, destroying user trust in the score evidence.
3. **Financial advice language** — Score reasoning contains "buy", "sell", "recommend", or directional investment advice instead of neutral analytical language, creating regulatory and reputational exposure.
4. **Indonesian term misidentification** — A Bahasa Indonesia financial term is incorrectly mapped to an English equivalent, producing a wrong dimension score (e.g., "pendapatan komprehensif lain" treated as core revenue).
5. **Score silently null on quota exhaustion** — Gemini quota errors produce null scores without surfacing useful error context, making debugging and monitoring impossible.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Retail investment / Financial analysis (Indonesian capital markets — IDX)

**User Population:** Urban Indonesian professionals (mid-career, ages 25–45) who hold IDX equities or are considering them, but lack formal finance training. They can read English but cannot parse financial statements, accounting terminology, or Bahasa Indonesia PSAK disclosures. The 2024 OJK National Financial Literacy Survey puts Indonesia's financial literacy index at 65.43% with capital market literacy considerably lower; the IDX investor base reached 13.66 million in mid-2024 with the majority under 30. This population makes investment decisions by skimming app headlines and social media sentiment rather than primary source filings — Clarifin's score is often the first structured analytical signal they receive about a company.

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** The score and dimension reasoning are acted on by investors with limited ability to independently verify or sanity-check the output. A score that is wrong in a visible, confident way (e.g., scores a cash-burning company 8/10 on Profitability because it misread gross profit as net profit) can directly influence a capital allocation decision. The score is labeled "not financial advice" but will be treated as a strong signal by the target user. Incorrect scores erode trust irreversibly — Indonesian retail investors discuss platforms in WhatsApp groups and Stockbit forums; a single credible failure spreads quickly.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

```
Dimension: Core earnings quality (Profitability)
Good: Score reflects operating income and net profit margin from continuing operations,
      explicitly excludes extraordinary items (PSAK still permits extraordinary item
      disclosure unlike IFRS), and cites the multi-year margin trend from the income
      statement notes.
Bad: Score is based on gross profit alone, ignoring operating expenses and below-the-line
     items; or inflates profitability by treating "pendapatan komprehensif lain" (other
     comprehensive income) as core operating revenue.
Stakes: Critical
Source: CFA Institute — earnings quality framework; PSAK extraordinary items disclosure
        (retained in PSAK vs. eliminated in IFRS); practitioner knowledge from IDX
        fundamental analysis convention.
```

```
Dimension: Solvency and liquidity adequacy (Balance Sheet)
Good: Score references current ratio, debt-to-equity ratio, and interest coverage ratio
      from the audited balance sheet (Laporan Posisi Keuangan); notes whether debt is
      predominantly short-term vs. long-term; flags if the company is in a capital-intensive
      sector where higher leverage is normal (e.g., banking, property, toll roads).
Bad: Score treats total liabilities as uniformly bad without distinguishing operating
     liabilities (accounts payable) from financial debt; or scores a bank poorly for high
     leverage without recognising that high leverage is structurally normal for financial
     institutions under OJK capital adequacy rules.
Stakes: Critical
Source: IDX sector-adjusted ratio norms; OJK prudential regulation for financial sector
        issuers; practitioner knowledge from Indonesian equity analyst reports.
```

```
Dimension: Revenue and earnings trajectory (Growth Trend)
Good: Score covers at least 3 years of revenue and net income trend using comparative
      figures from the filing (IDX annual reports mandatorily include 3-year comparatives
      under PSAK); distinguishes organic growth from growth via acquisition or revaluation;
      notes year-over-year change in percentages the non-finance user can interpret.
Bad: Score compares only current year to prior year (single data point), missing the
     multi-year slope; or treats a one-time asset revaluation gain as evidence of earnings
     growth; or flags growth in "laba ditahan" (retained earnings) as revenue growth.
Stakes: High
Source: IDX listing rules requiring 3-year comparative statements; CFA growth analysis
        framework; practitioner knowledge of common misreads in automated financial parsing.
```

```
Dimension: Price reasonableness relative to fundamentals (Valuation Context)
Good: Score notes whether the P/E and P/B ratios cited are above or below the sector
      median for IDX-listed companies in the same industry; frames valuation as "relatively
      expensive" or "relatively cheap" compared to a stated benchmark, without concluding
      whether the user should buy or sell; sources the ratios from the document where
      available or notes they require live market data (Phase 9).
Bad: Score assigns a high valuation sub-score purely because the stock has risen recently
     (price momentum conflated with fundamental value); or uses absolute P/E thresholds
     from US market norms (S&P 500 historical ~17x) applied directly to IDX companies
     without sector or emerging market adjustment; or makes any buy/sell directional
     statement.
Stakes: High
Source: OJK Regulation Kep-26/PM/1996 on investment advisors; practitioner knowledge of
        IDX sector valuation norms; CFA relative valuation methodology.
```

```
Dimension: Citation traceability (all dimensions)
Good: Every sub-score reasoning sentence references at least one specific page number from
      the uploaded document; the quoted snippet on that page visibly supports the claim
      made (a reviewer can open the PDF to that page and confirm the logic).
Bad: Reasoning references "the financial statements" without a page number; or cites page
     N where that page is a cover page, table of contents, or auditor signature page that
     contains no relevant financial data; or the quoted snippet is paraphrased rather than
     verbatim.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Clarifin product requirement SCORE-04; practitioner standard that unsourced
        analytical claims are inadmissible in professional equity research.
```

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **One-time item inflation.** PSAK retained the concept of "extraordinary items" (pos luar biasa) that IFRS eliminated. A large one-time gain (asset sale, insurance settlement, government subsidy recognition) can inflate net profit dramatically in a single year. An AI that reads the bottom-line profit figure without examining the notes section will produce an inflated Profitability score for a company whose core business is actually deteriorating. Indonesian retail investors are disproportionately misled by this because they follow headline EPS, not adjusted EPS.

2. **PSAK/IFRS terminology collision.** As of 2024, Indonesia introduced PSAK International (direct IFRS adoption), meaning some IDX filings use PSAK numbering (PSAK 1, PSAK 7) while others use the new i-prefix convention (PSAK i101, formerly PSAK 1). Older filings still reference legacy PSAK. An LLM may misclassify line items when the same concept has different labels across filing vintages — e.g., "Ekuitas yang dapat diatribusikan kepada pemilik entitas induk" (equity attributable to owners of the parent) being misread as total equity, understating leverage in a consolidated group with significant non-controlling interests.

3. **Banking and financial-sector leverage misread.** Approximately 30% of IDX market cap is in banking and financial services. These entities carry legally mandated high leverage (deposits as liabilities). An AI applying generic debt-to-equity thresholds will consistently score Indonesian banks poorly on Balance Sheet even when they are well-capitalised under OJK's KPMM (Capital Adequacy Ratio) framework. The score must either suppress the leverage penalty for financial-sector issuers or note sector context explicitly.

4. **Revenue label ambiguity in Bahasa Indonesia.** The word "pendapatan" means revenue in most contexts but also appears in "pendapatan bunga" (interest income), "pendapatan komprehensif lain" (other comprehensive income), and "pendapatan non-operasional" (non-operating income). An LLM that sums all lines containing "pendapatan" will produce materially inflated revenue figures, inflating both Profitability and Growth Trend scores. This was identified as a high-risk translation pair in the project's own Bahasa Indonesia eval harness (CLAUDE.md §2.5).

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

**OJK Investment Advice Prohibition (Kep-26/PM/1996 and successor regulations):**
OJK's definition of "Penasihat Investasi" (Investment Advisor) is broad. Any entity that, for compensation or on a regular basis, gives advice regarding the purchase or sale of securities is subject to OJK licensing requirements. Clarifin avoids this by:
(a) producing analysis and explanation only, not buy/sell recommendations,
(b) labeling all output "AI Assessment · not financial advice" (SCORE-06, DISCLAIM-01),
(c) hard-coding no-recommendation instructions in every LLM system prompt (DISCLAIM-02).
The score sub-dimension reasoning must use neutral analytical language ("the company's margins have declined", "debt levels are elevated relative to sector peers") and must not contain directional investment verbs ("buy", "sell", "accumulate", "avoid", "underweight", "overweight").

**OJK AI Governance for Financial Services (2024–2025):**
OJK Regulation No. 3/2024 on Technological Innovation in Financial Services (ITSK) established a licensing regime for AI-powered financial tools including robo-advisory and digital financial planning services. OJK also published AI Governance Guidelines for the banking sector in 2025 emphasising consumer protection, model reliability, and data transparency. Clarifin operates as a document analysis tool, not a registered financial service provider, but should maintain standards consistent with OJK's AI ethics principles: explainability (citations satisfy this), consumer protection (disclaimers), and model reliability (schema validation and eval harness).

**No GDPR / HIPAA equivalent identified.** Indonesian data protection is governed by UU PDP (Personal Data Protection Law, effective 2024) but financial document analysis of public company filings does not involve personal data and is not in scope.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** `@google/genai` — Google Generative AI SDK (provider-native)

**Version:** `^1.52.0` (already installed in project)

**Rationale:**
The project is a TypeScript/Next.js monorepo already using `@google/genai` directly for explanation generation in `generate-explanation.ts`. Score generation is functionally identical — a single structured output call with `responseMimeType: "application/json"`, `responseSchema`, stream accumulation, and Zod parse. Introducing a second generation SDK (Vercel AI SDK) for an identical call pattern would add dependency surface, diverge from the established code style, and create two competing generation abstractions in the same pipeline with no offsetting benefit at this scale. All necessary infrastructure (Gemini Files API upload, `waitForFileReady`, retry logic, Supabase upsert pattern) is already implemented in the sibling `generate-explanation.ts` file and can be mirrored directly. This is not a RAG pipeline, multi-agent system, or chatbot — it is a single deterministic generation call with a strict schema; no framework abstraction is warranted beyond the direct SDK.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Vercel AI SDK `generateObject` (`@ai-sdk/google`) | Explicitly deferred in CONTEXT.md D-04: consistency with existing `@google/genai` codebase takes precedence over ergonomic API; no free-tier advantage; may be revisited in Phase 11 if adopted for observability tracing |
| LangChain / LlamaIndex | Abstraction overkill for a single structured generation call; adds dependency weight and LangChain version churn risk; CLAUDE.md explicitly warns against LangChain for this use case |
| Google ADK | Python-only (Java/Python); project is TypeScript-first |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Yes — the project is already committed to Google Gemini as primary LLM provider (CLAUDE.md §2). Score generation uses the same provider and SDK as explanation generation; this is a deliberate consistency choice, not an accidental lock-in.

### Entry Point Pattern

Minimal copy-paste runnable pattern for `generate-score.ts`. This mirrors `generate-explanation.ts` exactly — only the schema import, prompt call, and return type change.

```typescript
import "server-only";

import { createPartFromUri, GoogleGenAI } from "@google/genai";
import { env } from "@/lib/env";
import { SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA, scoreSchema, type ScoreResult } from "./score-schema";
import { SCORE_MODEL_ID, buildScorePrompt } from "./score-prompts";
// Re-use helpers already defined in generate-explanation.ts
import { isIndonesianDoc, waitForFileReady, uploadFresh } from "./generate-explanation";

export async function generateScore(params: {
  docId: string;
  pdfBytes: Uint8Array | null;
  filename: string;
  totalPages: number;
  extractionSource: string | null;
  fileResourceName: string | null;
  firstPageText: string;
}): Promise<{ result: ScoreResult; fileResourceName: string }> {
  const ai = new GoogleGenAI({ apiKey: env.GEMINI_API_KEY });
  let resourceName: string;
  let uri: string;
  let mimeType: string;

  // 1. Resolve Files API resource — reuse cached or upload fresh
  //    (exact mirror of generate-explanation.ts lines 143–167)
  try {
    if (params.fileResourceName) {
      const ready = await waitForFileReady(ai, params.fileResourceName);
      resourceName = params.fileResourceName;
      uri = ready.uri;
      mimeType = ready.mimeType;
    } else {
      if (!params.pdfBytes) throw new Error("generateScore: no fileResourceName and no pdfBytes.");
      const fresh = await uploadFresh(ai, params.pdfBytes, params.filename);
      resourceName = fresh.resourceName;
      uri = fresh.uri;
      mimeType = fresh.mimeType;
    }
  } catch {
    // FAILED / expired path — re-upload if bytes available
    if (!params.pdfBytes) throw new Error("generateScore: file expired and no pdfBytes to re-upload.");
    const fresh = await uploadFresh(ai, params.pdfBytes, params.filename);
    resourceName = fresh.resourceName;
    uri = fresh.uri;
    mimeType = fresh.mimeType;
  }

  // 2. Build prompt
  const isIndonesian = isIndonesianDoc(params.extractionSource, params.firstPageText);
  const prompt = buildScorePrompt(params.totalPages, isIndonesian);

  // 3. Stream + accumulate — do NOT parse per-chunk (Pitfall 1)
  const stream = await ai.models.generateContentStream({
    model: SCORE_MODEL_ID,
    contents: [createPartFromUri(uri, mimeType), { text: prompt }],
    config: {
      responseMimeType: "application/json",
      responseSchema: SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA,
      thinkingConfig: { thinkingBudget: 0 }, // disable thinking for deterministic extraction
    },
  });

  let accumulated = "";
  for await (const chunk of stream) {
    accumulated += (chunk as { text?: string }).text ?? "";
  }

  if (!accumulated) throw new Error("Empty Gemini score response.");

  // 4. Defensive markdown fence strip + parse + Zod validate
  const stripped = accumulated
    .trim()
    .replace(/^```(?:json)?\s*/i, "")
    .replace(/\s*```$/i, "");

  const parsed = JSON.parse(stripped) as unknown;
  const result = scoreSchema.parse(parsed); // ZodError on invalid shape → caught by caller

  return { result, fileResourceName: resourceName };
}
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `GoogleGenAI` | Root SDK client, initialized with `apiKey` | Always — one instance per function invocation; the entry point for `ai.models` and `ai.files` namespaces |
| `ai.models.generateContentStream` | Streams response tokens as an async generator; returns partial JSON fragments | All structured output calls — accumulate the full string before `JSON.parse`; never parse individual chunks |
| `responseMimeType: "application/json"` + `responseSchema` | Constrains Gemini output to a JSON Schema; eliminates prose wrappers and markdown fences | Every structured extraction call; both fields must be set together to activate constrained decoding |
| `createPartFromUri(uri, mimeType)` | Creates a `Part` referencing a Files API file by its `https://` hosted URI | Passing the full PDF to the model without re-uploading when `gemini_file_resource_name` is already cached in `documents` table |
| `ai.files.get({ name })` | Retrieves a file's metadata including `state` (`PROCESSING`, `ACTIVE`, `FAILED`) and live `uri` | Inside `waitForFileReady` — must confirm `ACTIVE` and extract the `uri` before constructing the Part; `name` and `uri` are different fields |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Parsing chunks instead of the accumulated string.** Each chunk from `generateContentStream` contains a fragment of the final JSON — `chunk.text` is not valid JSON on its own. Calling `JSON.parse(chunk.text)` inside the `for await` loop will throw on every chunk except potentially the last. The accumulated string is the only safe parse target. This is the most common mistake when adapting streaming patterns to structured output.

2. **Confusing `file.name` (resource name) with `file.uri` (hosted URL) in `createPartFromUri`.** `ai.files.upload` returns an object where `name` is the resource identifier (e.g. `"files/abc123"`) and `uri` is the HTTPS URL (e.g. `"https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/files/abc123"`). `createPartFromUri` requires the `uri` field. Passing `name` directly produces a 400 error with a non-obvious message. Always call `ai.files.get({ name })` to retrieve the live `uri` before constructing the Part.

3. **Not checking for `FAILED` file state before generating.** Gemini Files API processes PDFs asynchronously. A file can reach `FAILED` if the PDF is corrupt, DRM-protected, or the upload was interrupted mid-stream. If `waitForFileReady` only polls for `ACTIVE` without explicitly throwing on `FAILED`, `generateContentStream` receives a broken file reference and returns an empty response with no diagnostic message. The existing `waitForFileReady` in `generate-explanation.ts` already handles this — do not omit the `FAILED` check when re-implementing.

4. **Omitting `thinkingConfig: { thinkingBudget: 0 }` for `gemini-2.5-flash`.** For `gemini-2.5-flash`, thinking mode is active by default (non-zero budget). For deterministic structured extraction with a fixed schema, thinking adds 100–400ms latency and extra token cost with no benefit — the `responseSchema` already constrains the output. Set `thinkingBudget: 0` explicitly. Note: `gemini-2.5-pro` does not support `thinkingBudget: 0` (minimum is 1024 tokens); the flash model has no such restriction.

5. **Zod schema and `SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA` drifting out of sync.** The Zod schema controls TypeScript types and runtime validation; `SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA` controls what Gemini generates. If you add a field to the Zod schema but not the JSON Schema, Gemini will never generate it and every Zod parse will fail. If you add a field to the JSON Schema but not Zod, parse passes but the TypeScript type is wrong. Keep both definitions co-located in `score-schema.ts` and update them together. The `explanation-schema.ts` file is the canonical example of this pattern.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

### Model Configuration

```typescript
// src/lib/explain/score-prompts.ts

export const SCORE_MODEL_ID = "gemini-2.5-flash";

// Passed verbatim in the config block of generateContentStream.
// responseSchema is imported from score-schema.ts; not inlined here.
export const SCORE_GENERATION_CONFIG = {
  responseMimeType: "application/json" as const,
  // responseSchema: SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA  — set at call site with the import
  thinkingConfig: { thinkingBudget: 0 },
  // maxOutputTokens: NOT set — schema bounds output to ~400–700 tokens;
  // an explicit cap risks truncating the final snippet array.
  // temperature: NOT set — defaults to 1.0 for gemini-2.5-flash with structured output;
  // acceptable for extraction tasks with a constraining schema.
};
```

Why `thinkingBudget: 0`: score generation is structured extraction with a fixed schema. Thinking mode adds latency and token cost without improving extraction accuracy when output is already constrained by `responseSchema`. `generate-explanation.ts` does not set this because explanations are open-ended prose — different tradeoff.

Why no `maxOutputTokens`: the score schema output is bounded by its own structure — 4 dimensions x (name + integer score + one-sentence reasoning + 2–3 snippets) produces approximately 400–700 output tokens. Setting an explicit cap risks cutting off the final snippet. Let the schema act as the natural bound.

### Core Pattern

`generate-score.ts` mirrors `generate-explanation.ts` line-for-line. The three substitutions are: the schema import (→ `score-schema.ts`), the prompt builder (→ `score-prompts.ts`), and the return type (→ `ScoreResult`). The Files API resolution block, stream accumulation loop, and fence-strip pattern are identical.

**Orchestration integration in `analyze-document-batch.ts`:**

After the existing step 8 (explanation upserted), insert before step 9 (status → ready):

```typescript
// Step 8b — Score cache check (D-03: skip if already scored)
const scoreCacheRes = await supabaseAdmin
  .from("document_analysis")
  .select("score")
  .eq("doc_id", docId)
  .maybeSingle();

if (scoreCacheRes.data?.score == null) {
  // Step 8c — Generate score with 1 retry on Zod failure (D-05)
  let scoreGenResult: GenerateScoreResult | null = null;
  for (let attempt = 1; attempt <= 2; attempt++) {
    try {
      scoreGenResult = await generateScore({
        docId,
        pdfBytes,
        filename: doc.filename ?? "document.pdf",
        totalPages: doc.total_pages ?? 200,
        extractionSource: doc.extraction_source,
        fileResourceName: fileResourceName, // use updated resource name from explanation step
        firstPageText,
      });
      break; // success — exit retry loop
    } catch (err) {
      const isZodError = err instanceof Error && err.name === "ZodError";
      if (!isZodError || attempt === 2) {
        // Non-Zod errors surface immediately; 2nd Zod failure exhausts retries
        console.error(`[score] attempt ${attempt} failed:`, err);
        break;
      }
      console.warn(`[score] Zod parse failure on attempt ${attempt}, retrying…`);
    }
  }

  if (scoreGenResult) {
    // Step 8d — Upsert score columns (D-02: soft-fail if upsert errors)
    await supabaseAdmin
      .from("document_analysis")
      .upsert(
        {
          doc_id: docId,
          score: scoreGenResult.result.overall_score,
          score_breakdown: scoreGenResult.result,
          score_at: new Date().toISOString(),
        },
        { onConflict: "doc_id" },
      );
    // Upsert errors are not surfaced — score null is acceptable per D-02
  }
  // If scoreGenResult is null: score stays null, document still transitions to ready (D-02)
}

// Step 9 — Transition to ready (unchanged from current code)
await supabaseAdmin.from("documents").update({ status: "ready" }).eq("id", docId);
```

### State Management

Score is cached per-document in two columns of `document_analysis`:
- `score` (number | null): the `overall_score` integer, used for fast SELECT in document list views
- `score_breakdown` (Json | null): the full `ScoreResult` object, used by the score card UI

If `score` is non-null for the given `doc_id`, generation is skipped entirely (D-03). Score is never regenerated unless the row is manually cleared. The `score_at` timestamp records when generation ran. No `score_reasoning` column is used — reasoning is embedded per-dimension inside `score_breakdown`.

### Context Window Strategy

Gemini 2.5 Flash has a 1,000,000-token context window. A 200-page IDX annual report passed as a Files API PDF reference consumes approximately 80,000–150,000 tokens at Gemini's native tokenization rate (including images, tables, and scanned pages). The score prompt adds ~800 tokens. Score output is ~400–700 tokens. Total context for a median 100-page document: ~85,000 tokens — well within the 1M ceiling.

No chunking, RAG, or page selection is used for score generation. The full PDF is passed as a single File Part. This is the correct architecture for a holistic score: the four dimensions (Profitability, Balance Sheet, Growth Trend, Valuation Context) require cross-referencing the income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement, and notes sections — page selection would introduce bias by missing relevant cross-section relationships.

If `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` with a context-length signal is returned: log to Langfuse with `doc_id` and `total_pages`, soft-fail score to null, do not retry. Do not implement truncation in v1.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Cross-cutting patterns specific to `@google/genai` v1.52.0 + Structured Data Extraction on IDX financial PDFs.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Zod

`@google/genai` does not accept Zod schemas directly. The pattern — established in `explanation-schema.ts` — requires two parallel definitions in a single file: a Zod schema for TypeScript inference and runtime validation, and a raw JSON Schema object for the `responseSchema` config field. Both must stay in sync. The JSON Schema controls what Gemini generates; Zod validates what arrives.

**`score-schema.ts` — complete, copy-paste ready:**

```typescript
import { z } from "zod";

// -----------------------------------------------------------------------
// Zod schema — TypeScript type inference and runtime validation
// -----------------------------------------------------------------------

const snippetSchema = z.object({
  text: z.string().min(1),             // verbatim quoted text from the document
  page: z.number().int().positive(),   // 1-based page number
});

const dimensionSchema = z.object({
  name: z.string().min(1),             // e.g. "Profitability"
  score: z.number().int().min(1).max(10),
  reasoning: z.string().min(1),        // one sentence, neutral language, no buy/sell
  snippets: z.array(snippetSchema).min(1).max(3),
});

export const scoreSchema = z.object({
  overall_score: z.number().int().min(1).max(10),
  dimensions: z.array(dimensionSchema).length(4),
});

export type ScoreResult = z.infer<typeof scoreSchema>;
// ScoreResult shape:
// {
//   overall_score: number;
//   dimensions: Array<{
//     name: string;
//     score: number;
//     reasoning: string;
//     snippets: Array<{ text: string; page: number }>;
//   }>;
// }

// -----------------------------------------------------------------------
// Raw JSON Schema — passed directly to @google/genai responseSchema config
// Field ordering mirrors scoreSchema above. Both must be updated together.
// -----------------------------------------------------------------------

export const SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA = {
  type: "object",
  properties: {
    overall_score: {
      type: "integer",
      minimum: 1,
      maximum: 10,
      description: "Overall assessment score from 1 (very poor) to 10 (excellent).",
    },
    dimensions: {
      type: "array",
      minItems: 4,
      maxItems: 4,
      items: {
        type: "object",
        properties: {
          name: {
            type: "string",
            description: "One of: Profitability, Balance Sheet, Growth Trend, Valuation Context.",
          },
          score: {
            type: "integer",
            minimum: 1,
            maximum: 10,
          },
          reasoning: {
            type: "string",
            description:
              "One sentence of neutral analytical reasoning. Must not contain buy, sell, or investment recommendations.",
          },
          snippets: {
            type: "array",
            minItems: 1,
            maxItems: 3,
            items: {
              type: "object",
              properties: {
                text: {
                  type: "string",
                  description: "Verbatim quoted text from the document.",
                },
                page: {
                  type: "integer",
                  minimum: 1,
                  description: "1-based page number where this text appears.",
                },
              },
              required: ["text", "page"],
            },
          },
        },
        required: ["name", "score", "reasoning", "snippets"],
      },
    },
  },
  required: ["overall_score", "dimensions"],
} as const;
```

**Retry logic — 1 retry on Zod failure, soft-fail to null on exhaustion (D-05):**

```typescript
// Inline in analyze-document-batch.ts (see §4 Core Pattern for full context)

for (let attempt = 1; attempt <= 2; attempt++) {
  try {
    scoreGenResult = await generateScore(params);
    break;
  } catch (err) {
    const isZodError = err instanceof Error && err.name === "ZodError";
    if (!isZodError || attempt === 2) {
      // Log what to diagnose: raw accumulated string (first 500 chars) + ZodError message
      // In production: log to Langfuse as a score_parse_failure event
      console.error(`[score] attempt ${attempt} terminal failure:`, err);
      break;
    }
    console.warn(`[score] Zod parse failure attempt ${attempt}, retrying:`, err);

// scoreGenResult null → score column stays null → document still reaches "ready" (D-02)
```

Log on Zod retry failure: the raw `accumulated` string (truncated to 500 chars) and the ZodError message. This is the minimum needed to diagnose schema drift between `scoreSchema` and `SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA`.

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

`@google/genai` is fully async. `generateContentStream` returns a `Promise<AsyncGenerator<GenerateContentResponse>>` — `await` the initial call to obtain the generator, then use `for await...of` to consume chunks.

**The one common mistake in Next.js App Router:** do not wrap `generateScore` in a `new Promise()` constructor or use synchronous bridges. The App Router execution context is already async. Call `await generateScore(...)` directly from the `async function runAnalyzeBatch` in `analyze-document-batch.ts`. This is identical to how `generateExplanation` is called — no special bridging needed.

**Stream vs. await for structured output:** use `generateContentStream` (streaming accumulation), not `generateContent` (non-streaming), even though the score result is not streamed to the browser. The reason: `generateContent` on a large PDF input waits for the full response before returning, which can cause the Vercel function to appear hung during long generation windows. Streaming accumulation returns the first token faster and keeps the function active through the response. The result is identical — the full accumulated string is parsed once at the end.

**Accessing chunk text safely:** `chunk.text` is a convenience getter that returns `undefined` if the chunk has no text part. Use `(chunk as { text?: string }).text ?? ""` — identical to the pattern in `generate-explanation.ts` line 184. Do not access `chunk.candidates[0].content.parts[0].text` directly; the getter handles the null-check internally.

### 4b.3 Prompt Engineering Discipline

**System vs. user prompt:** `@google/genai` `generateContentStream` accepts an optional top-level `systemInstruction` string in the config. For score generation, all instructions are in the single `contents` text Part — no separate system instruction. This matches the existing pattern in `generate-explanation.ts` (no `systemInstruction` set). Keeping the prompt self-contained simplifies the Langfuse trace (one prompt string to inspect) and avoids the interaction between `systemInstruction` and `responseSchema` which is not fully documented in v1.52.0.

**`buildScorePrompt` structure:**

```typescript
// src/lib/explain/score-prompts.ts

export const SCORE_MODEL_ID = "gemini-2.5-flash";

export function buildScorePrompt(totalPages: number, isIndonesian: boolean): string {
  const langNote = isIndonesian
    ? "The document is in Bahasa Indonesia. Translate all quoted snippets into English in the `text` field. Use correct English equivalents for PSAK financial terms (e.g. 'laba bersih' → 'net profit', 'aset lancar' → 'current assets')."
    : "";

  return `
You are a financial analyst reviewing an IDX-listed company financial document (${totalPages} pages).
${langNote}

Score this document on exactly 4 dimensions. Return the JSON object specified by the schema.

Dimension criteria:
1. Profitability — net profit margin, operating income, return on equity from continuing operations.
   Exclude extraordinary items (pos luar biasa). Do not count other comprehensive income as core profit.
2. Balance Sheet — current ratio, debt-to-equity, interest coverage. Note sector context
   (banks and financial institutions have structurally high leverage — do not penalise unless capital
   adequacy ratios are also stressed).
3. Growth Trend — year-over-year revenue and net income over at least 3 years of comparative data.
   Distinguish organic growth from one-time revaluations or acquisitions.
4. Valuation Context — P/E and P/B ratios where available in the document. Frame as "above/below
   sector norms" without directional investment advice. If ratios are not in the document, note that
   live market data is required and score conservatively (5).

RULES (non-negotiable):
- Do NOT use the words: buy, sell, invest, recommend, accumulate, avoid, underweight, overweight.
- All reasoning must be descriptive, not prescriptive.
- Page numbers must match actual document pages containing the cited data.
- overall_score is the mean of the 4 dimension scores, rounded to the nearest integer.
`.trim();
}
```

**Few-shot examples:** do not inline them. The `responseSchema` constrains the output structure more reliably than examples for a JSON extraction task. Examples add ~300–500 tokens of cost per call. If Zod validation failure rate exceeds 5% in Langfuse monitoring, add one few-shot example at that point.

**`maxOutputTokens`:** do not set. See §4 Model Configuration for rationale. The schema bounds output naturally.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management

Gemini 2.5 Flash has a 1,000,000-token context window. IDX annual reports are 80–250 pages; at Gemini's native PDF tokenization rate, this is 40,000–150,000 tokens. The score prompt adds ~800 tokens. Total context for a median 100-page document: ~85,000 tokens. No chunking or truncation is required.

**Why no page selection or RAG for scoring:** the four dimensions require cross-referencing multiple financial statement sections (income statement for Profitability, balance sheet for Balance Sheet, notes for Growth Trend, possibly a valuation summary section for Valuation Context). Selecting representative pages would require knowing in advance which pages contain each section — a solved problem for retrieval but not necessary when the full document fits comfortably in context. RAG is reserved for chat (Phase 10) where the user asks a specific question that maps to a retrievable passage.

**Citation accuracy at high page counts:** for documents over 150 pages, Gemini's ability to cite accurate page numbers decreases slightly. This is a known characteristic of long-context models. The prompt explicitly instructs accurate page citation, but this cannot be enforced by schema. The Phase 8 eval harness must include a citation spot-check for documents in the 150–250 page range.

**`RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` with context-length signal:** if this error occurs (unusual for IDX documents), log to Langfuse with `doc_id` and `total_pages`, soft-fail score to null. Do not implement truncation in v1 — page selection heuristics may miss critical financial sections and produce a less accurate score than null.

## 6. Guardrails

Guardrail classification follows the catastrophic/quality split from `ai-evals.md`: real-time blocking for failures that cannot reach the user, offline sampling for quality signals that inform improvement.

### Online (Real-Time)

These run synchronously inside `generateScore()` or immediately after it returns, before the result is persisted to `document_analysis`. Each adds minimal overhead (all are in-process string operations, < 1ms).

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| **Investment advice language block** | After stream accumulation, before Zod parse: scan the full `accumulated` string for any of the blocked terms (case-insensitive regex): `\b(buy|sell|invest|recommend|accumulate|avoid|underweight|overweight)\b`. Also checked post-parse against every `reasoning` and snippet `text` field. | If blocked term found in `reasoning` or `text`: log a `compliance_violation` event to Langfuse with the offending field and term; set `scoreGenResult = null`; document soft-fails to `score = null` per D-02. Do not surface the non-compliant output to the user under any circumstances. The Langfuse event enables manual review and prompt correction. |
| **Schema parse failure with retry** | Zod parse throws on the accumulated JSON. | Retry once (attempt 2). If second attempt also fails: log `score_parse_failure` event to Langfuse with first 500 chars of `accumulated` string and ZodError message; `scoreGenResult = null`; document soft-fails per D-02. Zod parse success rate is tracked as a production metric (see §7). |
| **Empty response guard** | `accumulated` is empty string after stream exhaustion. | Throw `"Empty Gemini score response"` immediately without retry (empty response indicates a quota or API error, not a parsing issue — retrying adds latency with low probability of success). Log to Langfuse with error type tag `"empty_response"`. |
| **Quota exhaustion / RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED detection** | Gemini API throws `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 429) or context-length exceeded error. | Log to Langfuse with `doc_id`, `total_pages`, and error code. Set `scoreGenResult = null`; soft-fail per D-02. Alert threshold: if 5 or more `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` events are logged within a single cron window, surface a Langfuse alert (see §7). |

### Offline (Flywheel)

These run in batch against sampled production traces in Langfuse. They do not affect live requests. Results feed prompt iteration and eval dataset expansion.

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Zod parse success rate** | 100% of production score generation traces (low volume at hobby scale — every event is reviewed) | If weekly parse success rate drops below 95%: inspect the most recent `score_parse_failure` traces in Langfuse; check for `scoreSchema` / `SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA` drift; update schema or add a few-shot example to `buildScorePrompt` |
| **Compliance term detection rate** | 100% of traces (automated — the online guardrail already flags and logs every violation) | If any `compliance_violation` event appears: immediate prompt review; update blocked-term instruction in `buildScorePrompt`; re-run eval harness against fixtures 3, 7, and 13 before next deploy |
| **Citation page plausibility** | Random 10% sample of generated scores in production, reviewed monthly by developer opening cited pages in source PDFs | If sampled citation spot-check finds >20% of cited pages are non-financial pages (covers, ToC, auditor signatures): add explicit citation instruction to prompt reinforcing "cite only pages containing income statement, balance sheet, or notes"; add adversarial citation fixture to reference dataset |
| **Profitability score inflation for distressed companies** | Monthly review of all documents where `overall_score >= 7` and the company is known to be cash-burning or in distress (cross-reference against dev knowledge of fixture 7 and any real-world GOTO/distressed filings) | If Profitability score > 5 for a document where net income is negative: inspect Profitability reasoning for OCI misclassification or extraordinary item inflation; add a targeted fixture to the reference dataset covering that failure mode |
| **Bahasa Indonesia term mapping accuracy** | 100% of score traces tagged `"bahasa-indonesia"` reviewed by developer; monthly bilingual review session for any new ID-primary document types encountered in production | If a new Bahasa Indonesia mistranslation is discovered (e.g., a new PSAK i-prefix term): add to the PSAK glossary injected by `buildScorePrompt`; update the eval blocklist in `scripts/eval/run-score-eval.ts`; add a fixture covering the new term |
| **Valuation Context score at 5 (data-absent default)** | Weekly: count what fraction of Valuation Context scores are exactly 5 in production | If >70% of Valuation Context scores are 5 (indicating the prompt conservative-default is always triggered, meaning ratios are rarely in documents): investigate whether the document set skews toward filings that don't include P/E or P/B; consider whether Phase 9 live stock data should feed back into the score in a future iteration |

**Key Metrics to Track:**

| Metric | How Tracked | Target |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Score generation success rate | `(traces with score != null) / (total score generation traces)` in Langfuse dashboard | ≥ 95% |
| Zod parse success rate (first attempt) | Count of traces where `zodParseSuccess = true` on attempt 1 vs. total | ≥ 97% |

| Score generation latency (p50, p95) | Langfuse generation `completionStartTime` → `endTime` | p50 ≤ 18s, p95 ≤ 35s |
| Quota exhaustion events | Count of `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` error traces per day | Alert if ≥ 5 in a single cron window |
| Null score rate (documents reaching `ready` with `score = null`) | Query `document_analysis` where `score IS NULL AND score_at IS NULL` vs. total `ready` documents | ≤ 5%; sustained > 5% triggers quota or prompt investigation |
| Bahasa Indonesia trace proportion | Count of traces tagged `"bahasa-indonesia"` / total | Informational — no target; track to ensure ID-source documents are being correctly identified |

**Alert Thresholds:**

These are checked manually via the Langfuse dashboard weekly during the hobby phase. Automated Langfuse alerts (available on free tier via email notification) should be configured for:

1. **Compliance violation alert** — Langfuse alert: any `compliance_violation` event. Notification: immediate email. Action: review trace in Langfuse, patch prompt, re-run compliance fixtures, deploy fix before next cron run.

2. **Quota exhaustion burst alert** — Langfuse alert: ≥ 5 `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` events within any 6-hour window. Notification: email. Action: check Gemini API console for quota status; if free tier (250 RPD) is exhausted, no action needed — documents will be scored on the next day's reset. If paid quota, investigate unusual traffic.

3. **Schema failure rate alert** — If weekly Zod parse failure rate exceeds 5% (tracked via manual dashboard review): investigate `score_parse_failure` traces; check for `scoreSchema` / `SCORE_RESPONSE_SCHEMA` drift; roll back prompt version in Langfuse if a recent prompt change is correlated.

4. **Null score rate alert** — If null score rate in `document_analysis` exceeds 10% over a 7-day window (checked via weekly Supabase query): investigate score generation failure pattern; cross-reference with Langfuse error traces.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

At hobby scale, volume is low enough that all score generation traces can be reviewed. As traffic grows, weight sampling toward higher-signal interactions:

1. **100% of compliance violation events** — always reviewed manually.
2. **100% of Zod parse failure events** — always reviewed; these are the primary schema health signal.
3. **100% of traces for documents identified as Bahasa Indonesia** (`isIndonesian = true`) — reviewed during the first 30 days of production to validate the PSAK term mapping before reducing to sampled review.
4. **100% of traces for documents over 150 pages** — citation accuracy degrades at high page counts; full review during the first month.
5. **Random 10% of remaining traces** — general quality sampling; rotate the sampled set monthly.
6. **100% of traces where `overall_score >= 8`** — high scores are higher trust-impact; spot-check that the reasoning supports the score and no OCI inflation or extraordinary item is driving it.

**Prompt versioning:** Each version of `buildScorePrompt` is tagged with a version string (e.g., `score-prompt-v1`) stored in Langfuse prompt management. When a prompt is changed, the old version is retained so that score quality before and after can be compared using Langfuse's trace comparison view.
